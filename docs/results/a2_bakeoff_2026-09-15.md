# Phase A2: bake-off of candidate brains (stage 21)

Plan §20.4. Every candidate goes through the same platform: apartment maps
with dirt, battery and dock (stage 16), the vacuum sensor preset with noise,
dropout, one tick of delay and drifting odometry (stage 17), the occupancy
map + coverage planner + needs arbiter as the engineered layer (stages
19-20), and the stage-18 benchmark: 6 unseen apartments (seeds 3000-3005)
x 3 sensor-noise repeats, 800 steps, battery drains in 500 ticks. The score
is the mean episode reward; coverage, collisions, dockings and survival are
reported next to it. Nothing is compared outside this table.

## Protocol

| aspect | setting |
|---|---|
| training maps | apartment seeds 100, 101, 102 (never in the test set) |
| layer during training and test | planner mode `needs` (coverage gradient, dock trips, calls) |
| evolution (worm family, rnn, ncp) | population 40, 25 generations, sigma 0.02, weights in [-1, 1], 20% elites, no crossover; dense topology with a 5% mutation mask; fitness = mean benchmark reward on the three training maps, one noise repeat |
| PPO | stable-baselines3 MlpPolicy on the bounded channels, 300k environment steps, n_steps 2048, batch 64, training maps round-robin, deterministic actions at test |
| scripted floors | Roomba-style controller bare (own docking by beacon); the stage-19 test driver under the same layer |
| seeds | evolution seed 0 / PPO seed 0 for the first pass; the Plan asks for >= 3 seeds per candidate before a decision (§20.4), see below |

Commands: `make evolve-a2 CANDIDATE=<name>`, `doomworm ppo`, then
`make benchmark-a2`; raw rows in `benchmark_a2_2026-09-15/`.

## Reading

1. **Only PPO beats the floor.** The Roomba-style controller scores 29.5;
   PPO reaches 49.7 with the best coverage (0.31) but dies in half the
   episodes (9/18) and collides 34 times: it cleans hard and docks badly.
   Every evolved brain is at or below the floor.
2. **The worm needs its curriculum.** From the dataset weights the connectome
   gets 9.9 with 192 collisions per episode; from the stage-12 food brain it
   gets 26.5 with 68 collisions and 16/18 survival, statistically tied with
   the floor (± 12.7 vs ± 12.6) but four times the collisions. Same as the
   stage-12 observation: harder tasks need a starting point.
3. **Real ≈ Random, Real < Free** (Plan §50 classes, budget-matched rows
   only): dense 17.7 > random 12.5 > real 9.9 > shuffled −1.3, all within
   one standard deviation of each other except shuffled. The stage-11
   advantage of the real wiring (collision-free driving) is gone under the
   vacuum sensor preset: the real connectome now collides the most (192)
   while the free topology learned to drive without touching anything
   (0.1 collisions) at the price of coverage (0.10).
4. **Train fitness does not transfer.** Shuffled trains to 32.4 and tests at
   −1.3; dense 34.4 -> 17.7; the curriculum worm 46.8 -> 26.5. Three training
   maps overfit; the test maps have different room layouts and furniture.
5. **The networks from scratch did not learn to drive.** RNN and NCP end with
   coverage 0.02-0.03 and zero collisions: starting from N(0, 0.1) weights
   with mutation sigma 0.02 the readouts stay near zero and the wheels barely
   turn; their train fitness (10-17) is exploration bonus and dock visits.
   This is an initialisation/budget artefact of the shared protocol, not a
   verdict on either architecture. A fair rerun needs a larger initial scale
   or sigma for them, or gradient training for NCP.
6. **The engineered layer carries most of the result.** The scripted driver
   under map + planner + arbiter scores 55.6, above every learned brain;
   the same worm scores 4.6 with the coverage planner alone and 19.6 with the
   arbiter (stage 20). The brain's job on this platform is local driving:
   avoid contact and follow the gradient.

## Decision (provisional, one seed)

Plan §17.1 said: if the real connectome is ahead on no axis by the end of
the platform work, the robot brain becomes the free mode and the worm stays
as the control. On this table the real connectome is ahead on no axis
(reward, coverage, collisions, survival), with or without curriculum, so
that clause applies. The recommended stack for A3 is the engineered layer
(map, coverage planner, needs arbiter, beacon docking) plus a learned
reactive brain; which learned brain is not settled: PPO leads on reward and
coverage but not on survival, the curriculum worm leads on survival with
poor collisions, the free topology is collision-free with poor coverage.
The Plan requires >= 3 seeds before a decision; the next step is seeds 1-2
for ppo, worm-from-stage-12, worm, worm_random, worm_shuffled and
worm_dense, and a corrected initialisation for rnn / ncp. The research
question (§50) reads, at this budget: **Real ≈ Random on the vacuum task,
Real < Free and Real < PPO**.

## Caveats

- One evolution seed per candidate so far; stage 11 showed spreads of ~10
  reward between seeds. No ordering claim until seeds 1-2 are run.
- The worm variants start from their untrained dataset-derived weights; the
  additional "worm from stage-12 brain" row carries 50 generations of
  earlier food training (curriculum) and is not budget-matched.
- PPO's budget (300k steps) is not the evolution's budget (3000 episodes x
  800 steps = 2.4M steps of evaluation); neither was tuned.
